# Wi-Fi: 2.4 GHz versus 5 GHz

## Summary
| | 2.4 GHz | 5 GHz |
|---|---|---|
| Range | Longer | Shorter |
| Through walls | Better | Worse |
| Maximum speed | Lower | Higher |
| Interference | More (neighbors, microwaves, Bluetooth) | Less |
| Channels | Few, and they overlap | Many more |

## When to recommend each
- **2.4 GHz:** devices far from the router or behind walls, smart-home devices, and equipment that only supports this band.
- **5 GHz:** devices near the router or in line of sight: TVs, game consoles, computers, video calls, and large downloads.

## Channels on 2.4 GHz
Channels 1, 6 and 11 do not overlap with each other. In a building with many networks, picking one of those three is usually better than leaving the channel on automatic if the automatic setting picks an in-between one. Use a Wi-Fi analyzer app to see which channels neighbors use and pick the least crowded of the three.

## One name for both bands
Many routers can use the same network name on both bands and let the device choose. It usually works, but some devices choose badly. If a device stays on 2.4 GHz while next to the router, splitting the names (for example "Home" and "Home-5G") lets you choose by hand.

## What to record
The band and channel in use, and whether the problem happens on only one band.
